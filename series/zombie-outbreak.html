What is real in this story (8 sourced facts)
  • Hospital emergency power must come back within 10 seconds of a grid failure (NFPA 110 and NFPA 99). NFPA ↗
  • Hospitals plan to stand alone for days on site fuel; about 96 hours is the common planning target. CMS ↗
  • City water needs electric pumps, plan on about one gallon per person per day, and water left in the pipes drains to the lowest open tap. Ready.gov ↗
  • Fill clean containers while the water still runs. CDC ↗
  • Insulin, many vaccines and some liquid antibiotics must stay between 36 and 46 degrees Fahrenheit. CDC ↗ FDA ↗
  • In a disaster you send scarce resources where they change the outcome (crisis standards of care). National Academies ↗
  • Clean a cut with clean water and soap, cover it, and watch for spreading redness, heat or pus. Mayo Clinic ↗
  • Scrub hands with soap for at least 20 seconds, before eating and after touching anything dirty. CDC ↗

Also real: Diesel sludge clogs filters, a fuel gauge float can lie (dip the tank with a stick), oxygen makes fire burn hotter, a V on the ground means “need help” to rescuers, and a standby generator gets about three cranks from its own battery.

What is real in this story (9 sourced facts)
  • A fire alarm panel's standby battery is built to run the system for 24 hours (NFPA 72). NFPA ↗
  • Unopened insulin keeps to its expiry date at 36 to 46 degrees Fahrenheit; out of the fridge most labels give it 28 days, and insulin that has frozen must be thrown out. FDA ↗
  • Never siphon fuel by mouth. Diesel breathed into the lungs causes chemical pneumonia. ATSDR ↗
  • Frozen water bottles keep a fridge or cooler cold during an outage. CDC ↗
  • Rats fit through gaps about half an inch wide; keep food in metal or tight lidded containers. CDC ↗
  • Plan on one gallon of water per person per day. Ready.gov ↗
  • One inch of rain on one square foot of roof is about 0.6 gallons. USGS ↗
  • Roof water must be treated before drinking: 8 drops of unscented household bleach per gallon of clear water, then wait 30 minutes. CDC rainwater ↗ CDC emergency water ↗
  • Thirst and tiredness are early signs of diabetic ketoacidosis, which can turn deadly within days without enough insulin. CDC ↗

Also real: Melting a pound of ice soaks up about 144 British thermal units, water and sludge settle under diesel (ASTM D975, ASTM D6469), what's under you matters more than what's on top when you sleep in the cold (US Army Survival Manual), a lead battery loses about a third of its cranking power at freezing, and untreated summer diesel starts to wax at around 10 to 20 degrees Fahrenheit.

What is real in this story (4 sourced facts)
  • A flame indoors makes carbon monoxide, which has no smell and no color. It brings headache and confusion, and it can kill people while they sleep. Never use a portable camp stove indoors, and never burn charcoal inside. CDC ↗
  • Wash any bite at once with soap and water for at least 15 minutes, then get it seen. WHO ↗
  • Unopened insulin keeps at 36 to 46 degrees Fahrenheit; out of the fridge most labels give it 28 days, and insulin that has frozen must be thrown out. FDA ↗
  • Without enough insulin the body burns fat for fuel and the blood turns acid (diabetic ketoacidosis). Heavy thirst, vomiting and fruity breath are warning signs, and it can turn deadly within days. CDC ↗

Also real: Summer diesel starts to throw wax crystals in a hard frost and the fuel filter clogs first (ASTM D975), blending in kerosene lowers the temperature where the wax comes out, bite wounds are usually left open or loosely dressed instead of stitched because closing them traps bacteria, and the small engine drinks about 2 gallons an hour on a light load.

What is real in this story (7 sourced facts)
  • A fully charged lead acid battery reads about 12.6 volts at rest, and one that sits slowly loses charge on its own. A battery left flat for long builds up sulfate on its plates. Battery University ↗
  • Most truck manuals say not to crank a starter for more than about 10 to 15 seconds at a time, then let it rest. Ram owner's manual ↗
  • Air in a diesel fuel line acts like a lock and starves the engine; the hand primer pushes the air out. USU Extension ↗
  • Tires lose about 1 to 2 pounds of pressure a month. GAO ↗
  • An underinflated tire flexes, heats up and can fail. Tire Industry Association ↗
  • Insulin in use can be kept at room temperature up to 86 degrees for about 28 days; it should never sit in a hot car, and clear insulin that looks cloudy should be thrown away. MedlinePlus ↗
  • Evaporation from a wet cloth keeps insulin a few degrees cooler than the air. Life for a Child ↗

Also real: Microbes grow where water settles at the bottom of a fuel tank and their sludge clogs filters, so you draw fuel from the top and let water settle out before you pour; a cold diesel needs its glow plugs warm before it will start; an engine pushed while it overheats can warp its cylinder head; and a big rig's fuel tank can hold 100 to 150 gallons.
