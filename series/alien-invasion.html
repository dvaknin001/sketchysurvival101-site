What is real in this story (15 sourced facts)
  • Window glass reflects the long wave infrared a heat camera sees, so it shows a mirror image, not what's behind the glass. Firehouse ↗
  • Bearings from two or more places can locate a radio transmitter (radio direction finding). Wikipedia ↗
  • Shut a valve fast and the water slams to a stop, sending a pressure spike through the pipe (water hammer). Close valves slowly. Practical Engineering ↗
  • A closed fridge keeps food cold about 4 hours without power. Toss food that sat at 40 degrees or higher for 2 hours or more. Ready.gov ↗
  • Store at least a gallon of water per person per day; in very hot weather the need can double. Ready.gov ↗
  • Heat exhaustion: heavy sweating, pale clammy skin, dizziness. Move somewhere cooler, apply cool wet cloths, offer sips of water. Weather.gov ↗
  • Never use a generator inside, even with doors and windows open. It makes carbon monoxide. CDC ↗
  • Sound moves through steel at about 5,940 meters a second, about 17 times faster than through air. Engineering ToolBox ↗
  • To stop bleeding, press firmly and don't peek. If blood soaks through, leave the cloth and add another on top. MedlinePlus ↗
  • Watch a cut for warmth, redness, swelling, pus or fever. MedlinePlus ↗
  • Full dark adaptation can take up to 30 minutes, and dim red light won't impair night vision. FAA ↗
  • 8 drops of 6 percent unscented bleach per gallon of clear water, wait 30 minutes. No faint chlorine smell? Repeat and wait 15 more. EPA ↗
  • One inch of rain on 1,000 square feet of roof is about 623 gallons. USGS ↗
  • Seconds from flash to thunder divided by 5 gives miles. If you can hear thunder, you're within striking distance. Weather.gov ↗
  • Knots weaken rope: a bowline keeps about 67 to 75 percent of its strength, a square knot about 43 to 47 percent. Practical Sailor ↗

Also real: Rolled oats are steamed before they're flaked, a phone checks in with the network even when you don't touch it, SOS is three short, three long, three short, and a light 700 feet up clears the horizon for about 32 miles.
